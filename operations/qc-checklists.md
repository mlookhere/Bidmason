# QC Checklists and the Approval Rule

The operating rule of the whole company:

**AI can create. AI cannot publish without approval.**

AI drafts websites, proposals, pricing, emails, and PDFs. But: the operator approves before a website publishes; the contractor approves before a proposal sends; the operator approves before core templates change; and no legal-contract language goes out as final without disclaimers or lawyer-reviewed templates. Present everything as a draft for approval. Never describe output as sent, published, or live.

## Must QC closely (flag these explicitly in every deliverable)
- Pricing (every number, and its basis)
- Scope of work
- Exclusions
- Guarantees (should be rare to none — see Risk Reviewer)
- Legal-sounding language
- Client name / business info
- Any claim of "licensed," "insured," "certified," "bonded," or "guaranteed"
- Final PDF before the client sends it
- Website before launch

When a deliverable contains any of these, end it with a **"Close QC required"** list pointing the operator at exactly those items.

## Light QC (present normally)
Blog posts, FAQs, service page drafts, follow-up email tone, monthly reports, formatting.

## Automated later (no approval gate once the system is proven)
Lead notifications, internal summaries, proposal draft generation, file naming, CRM record creation, reminder scheduling, onboarding checklists. Until the operator says the system is proven, treat these as light QC too.

## Self-check before presenting any deliverable
1. Did I invent any fact not in the brief or the user's input? (Fix: move to assumptions or Internal Note.)
2. Does anything client-facing mention AI or automation? (Fix: remove.)
3. Are all "must QC closely" items flagged?
4. Could the Inspector's relevant checklist fail this? (Run it mentally first.)
5. Is there a clear next action for the operator (approve / fix / send)?

## File naming convention
`{client-slug}-{deliverable}-v{YYYY-MM-DD}` — e.g., `pacific-shine-proposal-hoa-monthly-v2026-06-09.pdf`
