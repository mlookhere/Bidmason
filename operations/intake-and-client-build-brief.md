# Intake and the Client Build Brief

Every client starts here. Raw intake answers go in; an approved Client Build Brief comes out. The brief is the single source of truth every department reads — if a fact isn't in the brief, downstream agents must flag it rather than invent it.

## Intake form fields

Collect from the contractor (form, call notes, or email — any format):

1. Business name (exact legal/DBA spelling)
2. Services offered (their words)
3. Service area (cities/neighborhoods/radius)
4. License and insurance info (numbers and types if they'll share; at minimum yes/no per type)
5. Target customers (who they want more of)
6. Commercial jobs they want more of (specific: "HOA monthly contracts," "restaurant accounts")
7. Past jobs (3–5 representative, with rough values if shared)
8. Photos (before/after, crew, equipment)
9. Existing website / Google Business profile / social links
10. Pricing basics (hourly rates, per-unit rates, minimum job fee, typical job ranges)
11. Voice notes from walkthroughs (if they have any — instant Bid Writer fuel)
12. Anything they're proud of (years in business, big accounts, reviews)

Missing fields don't block the brief — they become an "Outstanding from client" list.

## Client Build Brief template

```
# Client Build Brief — {Business Name}
Status: DRAFT / APPROVED   Date: {date}   BidMason tier: Starter / Growth / Commercial Pro

## Business facts (verified)
Name, service area, years, crew size, license/insurance status — only what the client stated.

## Services
Lead services / supporting / one-time, in the client's vocabulary.

## Target commercial buyers
Who they want, ranked.

## Pricing basics
Rates, minimums, typical ranges. Mark gaps.

## Assets on hand
Photos, testimonials, logo, existing site. Mark gaps.

## Voice and personality notes
How the contractor talks; anything that should survive into the copy.

## Outstanding from client
What's missing and who's chasing it.
```

## Rules
- The operator approves the brief before step 2 (positioning) begins.
- Update the brief when new facts arrive; departments always read the latest version.
- Never promote an unverified claim (especially licensed/insured/certified) into "Business facts (verified)."
