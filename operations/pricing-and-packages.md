# Pricing and Packages

Two pricing layers exist in BidMason. Don't confuse them.

## Layer 1: What BidMason charges contractors

Do not underprice like a cheap tool. The goal is setup cash plus recurring revenue.

### Starter — $997 setup + $149/month
Small contractors. 5-page website, 3 proposal templates, basic quote form, branded PDF proposal format, 1 revision round, monthly hosting/support/reporting.

### Growth — $2,500 setup + $299/month (the main offer)
10-page website, 5 service/industry pages, proposal generator, follow-up email system, client intake form, pricing tier builder, monthly optimization.

### Commercial Pro — $5,000 setup + $499–$999/month
For contractors chasing property managers, HOAs, gyms, restaurants, medical offices, hotels, large recurring accounts. Full website, proposal system, CRM-lite dashboard, proposal analytics, multiple branded templates, custom service packages, quarterly sales asset refresh, more hands-on QC.

Default recommendation is **Growth**; route up to Commercial Pro when the contractor names large recurring accounts as the goal.

## Layer 2: The contractor's customer-facing pricing tiers

Built by the Offer Architect and Bid Writer for each client's services.

### Tier construction rules
- **Three options.** One feels light, one feels right, one feels premium. The middle tier is the intended choice — build it as the honest best fit, then construct the other two around it.
- **Frequency is the natural axis** for recurring commercial work: One-Time / Quarterly / Monthly. Per-visit price drops as frequency rises — recurring revenue is worth the discount.
- **Custom intervals are an option, not a failure.** When the interval is the only mismatch, keep the package structure intact and offer a **Custom Schedule** option — same scope, per-visit price computed from the same basis, frequency adjusted. Every program table carries a "need a different schedule?" line so the offer doesn't die on the interval.
- **Premium tier adds scope, not just frequency:** priority scheduling, extra areas, reporting/photos after each visit, day-porter add-ons — whatever the trade supports.
- **Name tiers by what they are** ("Monthly Maintenance Program"), not by metal ("Gold"). Commercial buyers procure programs, not medals.
- **Real numbers only when the brief has pricing basics.** Show the basis for every computed figure ("4 visits × 3 hrs × $85/hr"). Otherwise placeholder `$____` and an Internal Note item.
- **Respect the minimum job fee** if the brief lists one; flag any tier that prices under it.

### Pricing library (per client, grows over time)
Maintain in the client's records: service rates, margin targets, minimum job fees, recurring vs one-time package definitions. The Bid Writer reads this before pricing any proposal.
